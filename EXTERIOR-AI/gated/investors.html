For investors

Two neighbours buy the same windows.
One pays twice as much.

That is not a figure of speech. It is measured, from settled prices on real jobs, and it is the whole reason this company exists.

  • The wedge: windows and doors, priced from one photograph.
  • Revenue today: signed per-lead contracts with two national installers, at £100 and £130 per lead, up to three installers per homeowner.
  • The expansion: the same photograph prices five more trades.
  • The founder: twenty-four years selling windows for a national installer.

The problem, quantified

UK window and door retail runs on a discount ladder. The same product, on the same house, sells at a different price depending on how long the customer holds out on the night. Nobody publishes this, because it only works if the customer does not know.

Facet Pro's founder sold into that structure for twenty-four years. These are the settled prices he can evidence, against what the two installers actually quote:

Two products, two national installers, and the same shape both times: the floor sits just under the settled price and the ceiling sits at double it. The customer's negotiating stamina is worth more than the specification of the product.

The product shows both numbers. A homeowner sees what the job should cost, and beside it what the market would charge them for it. For a semi with eight casement windows that reads as £6,021 – £11,183 against a market range of £7,570 – £17,204.

What is hard about this

Pricing a house from a photograph requires knowing how big the house is. Facet Pro measures it against the one object of known size in almost every photograph of a British home: the front door. A UK front door is 1.98 m. Everything else — wall area, window count, window sizes, roofline length — is derived from that reference and the proportions of the image.

That is the part that took the domain knowledge, and it is the part that generalises. Once the house is measured, the marginal cost of pricing a second trade is close to zero, which is why one photograph prices windows, doors, cladding or render, fascias and guttering, the roof, and a conservatory.

It also creates a saving that is real rather than rhetorical. Scaffolding is a fixed cost. A homeowner doing windows this year and the roof next year pays for it twice.

How it makes money

Homeowners pay nothing. Installers pay for qualified leads: a homeowner who has chosen a specification, seen a price, and asked to be contacted.

  • Two signed contracts, at £100 and £130 per lead.
  • Three installers maximum per homeowner — a cap enforced in code, matching the consent wording, not a commercial preference.
  • Leads route by trade and postcode, so a render contractor is not sold a window job.

The lead is qualified in a way a call-centre lead is not: the person has already seen their own house with the product on it and a price attached, and has consented specifically to being contacted about it.

On the technology. The vision model is Anthropic's; the image generation is FLUX Kontext Pro, run on Replicate. Neither is proprietary and both are available to anyone. What is not available to anyone is knowing what a job settles at after twenty-four years of selling them, which measurements a price actually depends on, and having the installer contracts signed. The models are a component. They are not the moat, and any pitch that says otherwise will not survive a technical reader.

What the money is for

  • Calibration. Surveyed properties to validate the measurement against known wall areas — the difference between a method that works and a method that is proven to work.
  • Supplier rate cards across the size bands, so every trade is priced from evidence rather than judgement.
  • Installer supply. Two national contracts exist. Regional coverage needs more, and the routing to support them is already built.
  • Compliance. Legal completion and ICO registration, which gate revenue rather than product.

The question worth asking

Not whether a photograph can be turned into a render — it can, and several companies do it. The question is whether a render is worth anything without a price attached to it, and whether the price can be trusted.

Facet Pro is built by somebody who knows what these jobs actually cost and why two identical houses get different numbers. The visualiser is how a homeowner is persuaded to care. The price is the product.
